VO2max is the most oxygen your body can take in and use during hard effort. It is the standard measure of cardiorespiratory fitness, and it matters well beyond sport. In a Cleveland Clinic study of more than 120,000 adults, people with below-average fitness had a mortality risk similar in size to smoking or diabetes, compared with people of above-average fitness [1]. That is an association, not proof that fitness alone explains the difference, but it is one of the most consistent findings in exercise science.

The encouraging part is that fitness responds to training at any age. The first step is knowing where you start.

## Three tests you can do without a lab

Each test below turns a simple effort into an estimate of VO2max using an equation from a published validation study.

| Test | What you do | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cooper 12-minute run | Cover as much distance as you can in 12 minutes | People who already run comfortably |
| Rockport one-mile walk | Walk one mile as briskly as you can, then take your heart rate | Most active adults over 45 |
| Six-minute walk | Walk as far as you can in six minutes at your own pace | Returning after illness, surgery or a long break |

### The Cooper 12-minute run

Kenneth Cooper showed in 1968 that the distance covered in 12 minutes of running tracks treadmill-measured oxygen intake [2]. It is simple and repeatable, but it asks for a sustained hard effort, and it was developed in men. If running is not already part of your week, choose a walking test instead.

### The Rockport one-mile walk

This is the test I recommend for most people reading this. You walk one mile on a flat, measured route as quickly as you comfortably can, then record your time and your heart rate at the finish. In the original study of 343 adults aged 30 to 69, the estimate tracked measured VO2max closely [3]. It is demanding enough to be informative without asking you to run.

### The six-minute walk

The six-minute walk is the gentlest entry point. It is widely used in clinics because it lets people set their own pace. An equation that adds body weight, sex, resting heart rate and age to the distance walked explained most of the variation in measured VO2max in one study [4]. That study was small and its participants were younger than most of our readers, so read the number as a starting point rather than a precise score.

## How to test safely

- Warm up with five minutes of easy walking.
- Stop straight away if you feel chest pain or pressure, unusual breathlessness, dizziness or palpitations.
- If you have a heart or lung condition, or you have been told to limit exertion, check with your clinician before any timed test.
- If hard effort leaves you wiped out for a day or more afterwards, skip timed tests for now and talk with your clinician first.

> Pick one test and keep using it. The value is in the trend, not in a single number.
