## Body

Hello,

Your knee aches after a long walk, or your hip complains on the stairs. The natural move is to sit down and give it a few days. Sometimes that is the right call. Short rest is the right call for a hot, swollen joint, during a flare your clinician is managing, or when your surgical team has asked for it. The problem is long stretches of rest as the everyday default.

This week's article looks at why. When a leg is barely loaded for weeks, as after an ankle fracture, knee cartilage gets measurably thinner on scans. Cartilage has no blood supply of its own. Its nutrients reach it mainly from the fluid inside the joint, and lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that movement and loading help move that fluid into the cartilage.

The article is also clear about what exercise does not do. It has not been shown to regrow cartilage that has been lost. In people with or at risk of knee arthritis, exercise that loads the knee does not appear to harm cartilage. And international guidelines from OARSI list arthritis education and structured exercise as core treatments for knee and hip osteoarthritis, recommended for essentially everyone with the condition.

It ends with one small step: five minutes of gentle movement a day.

[Read the article](https://muscle-meta.com/blog/why-rest-is-the-wrong-prescription-for-a-sore-joint/)
