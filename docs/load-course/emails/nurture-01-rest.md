## Body

Hello,

When a joint hurts, resting it feels like the responsible thing to do. Sometimes it is. Short rest is the right call for a hot, swollen joint, during a flare your clinician is managing, or when your surgical team has asked for it. The problem is long stretches of rest as the everyday default.

Here is what long stretches of low loading look like. When a leg is barely loaded for weeks, as after an ankle fracture, knee cartilage gets measurably thinner on scans.

Cartilage has no blood supply of its own. Its nutrients reach it mainly from the fluid inside the joint, and lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that movement and loading help move that fluid into the cartilage. Avoiding activity is also linked with weaker muscles and more difficulty with daily tasks.

So the useful question is how much rest, and for how long, rather than rest or no rest.

Lesson 1 of Load as Medicine works through that question. It explains the joint fluid idea in more detail, then walks you through a five-minute daily routine for getting a stiff joint moving again.
