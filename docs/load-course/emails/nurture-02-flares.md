## Body

Hello,

Most people who start moving a sore knee or hip hit a bad day sooner or later. The joint is achier than usual the next morning, and the thought arrives straight away: "I've done something to it."

A flare is worth paying attention to. In most cases it is a sign that the last session asked a little more than the joint was ready for. That calls for adjusting the amount for a few days, not abandoning the plan.

This matters because exercise and education sit at the centre of the major international guidelines for knee and hip arthritis, ahead of medication. [CLAIM-C05] A plan that stops at the first flare never gets the chance to help.

Lesson 2 of Load as Medicine is about reading those signals. It covers the 5 out of 10 rule [CLAIM-C02], the next-day check, how far to scale back after a flare, and when a flare is a reason to see your clinician instead.
