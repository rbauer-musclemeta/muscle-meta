## Body

Hello,

Most people who start moving a sore knee or hip hit a harder day sooner or later. The joint is achier than usual the next morning, and the thought arrives straight away: "I've done something to it."

A flare is worth paying attention to. One harder day is information about the dose, not a sign you should stop moving. If the joint is not back to its usual after 24 hours, do less next time, for example fewer repetitions or a shorter session, until it settles. That is my clinical guidance, not a research-tested rule.

Sticking with movement matters because international guidelines from OARSI (2019) list arthritis education and structured land-based exercise as core treatments for knee and hip osteoarthritis, recommended for essentially everyone with the condition. A plan that stops at the first flare never gets the chance to help.

Lesson 2 of Load as Medicine is about reading those signals. It covers the acceptable pain guideline, which many clinicians use: an ache up to about 5 out of 10 during activity, back to your usual by the next day. That is clinical guidance, not a tested safety limit. The lesson also covers how far to scale back after a flare, and when a flare is a reason to see your clinician instead.

After surgery, follow your surgeon's or physical therapist's protocol. A hot, swollen joint, or one that gets stuck so you can't fully bend or straighten it, needs a clinician, not more exercise.

[See what lesson 2 covers](https://muscle-meta.com/courses/load-course/)
