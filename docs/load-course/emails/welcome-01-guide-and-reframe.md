## Body

Hello,

Many of us were told that arthritis is "wear and tear": that joints wear out like car tires, one mile at a time, until you are down to bone on bone.

That picture makes sense, and it is why so many people stop moving as soon as a joint aches.

Here is a more useful one. Cartilage, the smooth layer that covers the ends of the bones, is living tissue. It has no blood supply of its own. Its nutrients reach it mainly from the fluid inside the joint, and lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that movement and loading help move that fluid into the cartilage. Picture a sponge being gently squeezed and let go.

So the question changes. It stops being "how do I save my joint from use?" and becomes "how much use does my joint do well with?"

Some population data points the same way. In one pooled analysis, 3.5% of recreational runners had hip or knee arthritis, compared with 10.2% of non-runners. This is a link, not proof that running prevents arthritis. But it does not fit the idea that every step wears the joint down.

The guide you asked for puts movement to work after a joint replacement, once your surgical team has given you the go-ahead. If it is still unopened, it is a good place to start.

[Open your guide](https://muscle-meta.com/downloads/files/post-arthroplasty-loading-guide.pdf)
