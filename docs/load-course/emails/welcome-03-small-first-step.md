## Body

Hello,

When people decide to take a sore joint seriously, they often start big: a long walk, a new class, a full routine on day one. Then the joint flares, and the flare feels like proof that exercise was a mistake.

More often, the plan asked too much at once and the joint had no time to adjust.

That is why the first step in Load as Medicine is small on purpose. Five minutes of gentle movement through a comfortable range. Sit in a chair and slowly straighten and bend your knees, or stand holding a counter and make slow, easy hip circles. Sit or stand tall, with your weight centered, to help spread the load evenly through the joint. The aim is to get the joint moving, not to test it.

A small start does two jobs. It gives the joint regular movement, and lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that movement helps move joint fluid, which carries cartilage's nutrients, into the cartilage. It also gives you a run of days where moving went fine, and that record is a better answer to worry than any reassurance from me.

If five minutes feels easy for a week, add a little. If it does not, stay at five. Either way you are learning how your own joint responds.

After a joint replacement, do these movements only once your surgeon or physical therapist has given you the go-ahead. Their protocol comes first.

If you would like a starting point, the free joint-load check takes a few minutes. It starts with a short safety screen, then gives you a plain next step. Your answers stay in your browser on your device and are not sent to us or added to your email record.
