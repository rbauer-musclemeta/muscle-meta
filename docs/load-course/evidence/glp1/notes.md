# GLP-1 and joint health download: evidence notes (A1, 2026-11-02)

Evidence check for a lead-magnet download for adults 50 and over who take, or
are thinking about, a GLP-1 medicine. Source: Randy's report, copied unchanged
to `source-report.md`. The report gives no PMIDs and uses overstated language
("conclusively", "paradigm-shifting", "cartilage healing", "disease-modifying",
"definitive").

Claims are registered as C28 to C58 in `../claims-register.csv`, with the same
columns, statuses and Rule 8 grades as C01 to C27. Every PMID listed was read
in PubMed on 2026-11-02. Full texts read: the multi-society perioperative
guidance (PMC11666732).

## What the download may say

Use each row's safe wording. In short:

- In a large trial of adults with obesity and painful knee arthritis (STEP 9),
  weekly semaglutide plus diet and activity counselling led to about 14% weight
  loss over 68 weeks versus about 3% on placebo. Knee pain scores fell about 42
  points on a 100-point scale versus about 28 with placebo, and physical
  function improved more (C28 to C30). Both groups improved. The trial was in
  people with obesity and did not look at cartilage (C33).
- About 7 in 100 people stopped semaglutide because of side effects, mostly
  stomach-related, versus 3 in 100 on placebo (C58).
- About a quarter to a third of weight lost on these medicines is lean
  (fat-free) mass, a similar share to diet alone. Lean mass is not all muscle
  (C34). In diet-based weight-loss studies, adding strength training kept more
  lean mass (C35), and older adults often need more protein. A doctor or
  registered dietitian should set the amount (C36).
- Each pound of weight lost was linked with about four pounds less force on
  the knee per step while walking (C39). Weight loss is linked with lower risk
  of developing knee arthritis (C41), and losing more than about 5% of body
  weight improved function in trials (C42).
- One health-records study found fewer hip and knee replacements in the year
  after a GLP-1 prescription. This is a link only, and the same study found
  more new arthritis diagnoses in people without arthritis (C37).
- Lab and mouse studies suggest GLP-1 medicines may act on joint tissue
  directly. This has not been shown in people (C47, C48).
- If you take a GLP-1 and are planning surgery, tell your surgeon and
  anaesthesia team (C46).
- If you have had gout, tell your doctor before starting a GLP-1 (C44, as
  advice only).

## What the download must not say or imply

- **No dosing, and no advice to start, stop, change or choose a medicine.**
  No drug doses (semaglutide 2.4 mg, colchicine 500 mcg), no "stop 7 to 14
  days before surgery", and no comparison of drug classes. Nothing may read as
  recommending a drug.
- **No "heals cartilage", "rebuilds", "structural restoration",
  "disease-modifying" or "slows arthritis".** No human study shows any of
  these (C33, C47).
- **No causation from cohort data.** Do not say GLP-1s "prevent", "delay" or
  "reduce the need for" joint replacement (C37). Association only.
- **No protein number.** Do not print a gram-per-kilogram figure. The target
  belongs to a clinician or dietitian, and people with kidney disease may need
  less (C36).
- **No "mandatory" exercise prescription and no "walking is not enough".**
  Use the course's own exercise guidance, which A2 owns.
- **No "lean muscle" for lean mass**, and no "disastrous" or "catastrophic".
- **No early gout-flare claim and no uric-acid benefit claim.** Neither was
  supported (C44, C45).
- **No product, pipeline or regulatory claims**: 4P004, orforglipron, trial
  names or NCT numbers, FDA Fast Track, ACR 2026 (C52 to C54).
- **No health-economics figures** (C55).
- **No suggestion that results apply to people without obesity.** STEP 9
  enrolled people with obesity and a mean BMI of 40.
- The download ends with the medical disclaimer and points readers to their
  own doctor for every medicine question.

## The SGLT2 comparison, and the report's internal conflict

The report calls GLP-1s "paradigm-shifting" and says SGLT2 inhibitors "may
rival or exceed" GLP-1s for joints. The cohorts read here (Lund 2021, Preston
2024) found SGLT2 inhibitors were associated with less gout than GLP-1s, and
GLP-1s were not associated with less gout than matched controls. The report's
own TriNetX RA/OA claim was not found. A download cannot say that GLP-1s are
the best option for joints. It should not compare drug classes at all.

## Perioperative guidance: current position

The ASA's 2023 advice (hold weekly GLP-1s for a week before surgery) has been
replaced by the 2024 multi-society clinical practice guidance (Kindel et al.,
published in Surg Obes Relat Dis 39482213, Surg Endosc 39370500 and Clin
Gastroenterol Hepatol 39480373). It makes two recommendations:

1. Perioperative GLP-1 use should be a shared decision between the patient
   and the procedural, anaesthesia and prescribing teams.
2. Aspiration risk should be reduced through diet changes before the
   procedure and/or changes to the anaesthesia plan, such as rapid-sequence
   induction.

The guidance says it is consensus, not an evidence-based guideline, and it
aims to avoid cancelling procedures. Copy says only: tell your surgeon and
anaesthesia team that you take a GLP-1.
