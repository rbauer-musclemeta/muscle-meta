# Activity goals

Short guidance for each goal the reader can pick in the joint-load check
("What are you working toward?"). Shown in the results, after the next step,
one block per goal selected. Walking and running already has its line in

Audience: adults 50 and over. The source framework names pickleball players

may not yet have the strength for them, so the advice is to build a thigh and
hip strength base before adding more sport.

## Pickleball

Pickleball means quick starts, stops and side-to-side lunges, so your knees
and hips take load from many directions. Build a strength base in your thighs
and hips first, then add games a little at a time, and let the next-day check
tell you when to add more. *Randy Bauer, PT's clinical guidance.*

## Tennis

Tennis has the same quick changes of direction as pickleball, over a bigger
court. Start with strength work for your thighs and hips, then build court
time gradually, for example shorter sessions or doubles before singles.
*Randy Bauer, PT's clinical guidance.*

## Golf

The swing asks your hips and knees to turn and take your weight, and a full
round is a long walk. Start with range sessions or nine holes, and see how the
joint feels the next day before playing more. *Randy Bauer, PT's clinical
guidance.*

## Hiking

Downhill stretches tend to ask more of the knees than flat ground. Build time
on your feet on easier, flatter trails first, and try trekking poles on the
way down if they help you feel steadier. *Randy Bauer, PT's clinical
guidance.*

## Everyday life (stairs, standing, getting up from a chair)

These everyday tasks are the main goal for many people. Exercise is medicine
for the joint (C05, Established), and the most direct practice for these tasks
is the tasks themselves: a few chair sit-to-stands or a step-up on the bottom
stair, built up slowly. *Randy Bauer, PT's clinical guidance.*
