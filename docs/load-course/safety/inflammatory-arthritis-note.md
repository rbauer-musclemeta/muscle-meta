# Inflammatory arthritis note

For people living with rheumatoid arthritis or another inflammatory arthritis
(for example psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis, or gout). Shown:

- in the joint-load check results when the reader picks "Rheumatoid or other
  inflammatory arthritis" (the check continues; this is a note, not a stop);
- near the top of each download;
- once in the course, at the start of Lesson 2, and linked from Lessons 1 and 3;
- on the sales page, in the "Who this is for" section.

## Full version

### Heading

If you live with rheumatoid or another inflammatory arthritis

### Body

This course was built around osteoarthritis, the kind of arthritis often
described as "wear and tear". Rheumatoid arthritis and other inflammatory types work differently.
In these conditions the body's immune system drives swelling inside the joint,
and that swelling can come and go in flares.

Movement and strength work are often part of care for inflammatory arthritis
too [CLAIM-NEW-4]. But the right amount, and the right time to do it, depend
on how active the condition is, and that is something your rheumatology team
tracks.

So please:

- **use this course alongside your rheumatology team's advice, not in place
  of it;**
- **ask them whether the acceptable pain guide fits you.** It was written with
  osteoarthritis in mind;
- **during a flare,** when joints are hot, puffy, or stiff for a long time
  after you get up, check with your team before loading those joints;
- **stop and contact them** if a joint becomes hot and swollen and you also
  feel feverish or unwell.

## Short version (check results, cards, download header)

> **Rheumatoid or other inflammatory arthritis?** This course was built around
> osteoarthritis. Inflammatory arthritis flares differently, so use it
> alongside your rheumatology team's advice, not in place of it, and ask them
> whether the acceptable pain guide fits you. During a flare, check with them
> before loading a hot or swollen joint.
