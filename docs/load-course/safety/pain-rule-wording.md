# Pain-rule wording

The 5 out of 10 and 24-hour guideline, written as practical guidance. It is
always presented as clinical guidance, never as a law, a tested safety limit or
a guarantee.

Claim status: **C02, Unproven-Bridge.** Randy decided on 2026-11-02 to keep
the rule as labelled clinical guidance. Gilbert 2021 does not support it and is
never cited for it. The register gives no source to print beside the number;
the grade tag is the note.

## Name

Use **"the acceptable pain guideline"** in running copy. Where a short label is
needed (a heading, a tracker column), use **"Acceptable pain guide"**. Do not
call it a rule on its own, and never call it a "safe" level.

## Full version

For lessons, the tracker, the arthroplasty guide's introduction and the blog.

### Heading

How much ache is acceptable?

### Body

When a joint hurts, it is natural to read every ache as a sign of harm. Pain is
your body's alarm system. With arthritis, a moderate ache during exercise does
not by itself mean the joint is being damaged (C23, Promising).
Physical therapists often use a simple guideline to help people keep moving
without guessing.

**Rate the ache from 0 to 10.** 0 is no pain at all. 10 is the worst pain you
can imagine.

**During the activity: up to about 5 out of 10.** Many clinicians treat an ache
that stays moderate, at about 5 or below, as acceptable during exercise. If it
climbs above 5, ease off: go slower, use less weight, or stop that exercise for
today.

**By the next day: back to your usual.** Your usual is how the joint normally
feels on an ordinary day. Any extra ache should settle back to that within
about 24 hours. If it has, that amount of activity was a dose your joint
recovered from.

This is clinical guidance, not a tested safety limit for arthritis (C02,
Unproven-Bridge). In one study of people with knee or hip pain, the small rise
in pain after each exercise session shrank over 8 weeks (C02,
Unproven-Bridge).

**If it has not settled after 24 hours,** do less next time. Shorten the session,
lower the weight, or do fewer sets, and build back up once the joint is back to
its usual. One harder day is information about the dose, not a sign you should
stop moving.

### When the guideline does not apply

- **After surgery, your surgeon's or physical therapist's protocol comes
  first.** Use the pain levels and limits they give you, even if they differ
  from these.
- **A hot, swollen joint, or one that catches and gets stuck so you can't fully
  bend or straighten it, is not an "acceptable ache".** Stop and see a
  clinician (see the red-flag box).
- **Sharp, sudden or a new kind of pain** is a reason to stop that activity,
  even if it scores below 5.
- **Rheumatoid or other inflammatory arthritis:** this guideline was written
  with osteoarthritis in mind. Ask your rheumatology team whether it fits you
  (see the inflammatory arthritis note).
- **It does not replace advice from a clinician who knows your history.** If
  they have told you something different, follow them.

## Tracker adjustment rules

For A4, replacing the source's "Red flags & adjustments" box. The second rule
replaces the unsourced "reduce your volume by 50%" (see `copy-fixes.md`, F29).

**Adjusting your next session**

- **Ache above 5 out of 10 during the activity:** next time, lower the
  intensity (slower, lighter) or shorten the time.
- **Not back to your usual after 24 hours:** next time, do less, for example
  fewer repetitions or a shorter session, until it settles. A reasonable
  starting point is to cut back to about half the time, distance or sets you
  did, then build back up over the following sessions. *This is Randy Bauer,
  PT's clinical guidance, not a research-tested rule* (C17, Unproven-Bridge).
- **Hot, swollen, catching or getting stuck, or any sign in the red-flag box:**
  stop and contact a clinician.

## Words to avoid with this guideline

- "safe signal", "safe zone", "the activity is safe": use "acceptable".
- "perfect loading dose", "exact dose": use "a dose your joint recovered from".
- "pain means your joint is getting fed" or "rebuilding": remove. The ache is a
  signal to watch, not proof of repair.
- "should never exceed 5" (from the source tool): contradicts "up to 5 is
  acceptable". Use the wording above.
- Any citation to Gilbert 2021 for the 5 out of 10 number.
- "push through": never.
