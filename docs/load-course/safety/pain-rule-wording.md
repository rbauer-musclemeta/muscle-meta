# Pain-rule wording

The 5 out of 10 and 24-hour guideline, written as practical guidance. It is
always presented as a guideline used in physical therapy, never as a law or a
safety guarantee.

Claim status: the numeric threshold is `[CLAIM-C02]`. The source course cites
Gilbert 2021 for it; A1 is checking whether that paper states the rule or
whether it is a clinical heuristic traced to other sources. Until the register
merges, the wording below treats it as a practical guideline only, which is
true whichever way the grade comes out. If A1 cannot source it at all, keep the
wording and drop any citation beside it.

## Name

Use **"the acceptable pain guideline"** in running copy. Where a short label is
needed (a heading, a tracker column), use **"Acceptable pain guide"**. Do not
call it a rule on its own, and never call it a "safe" level.

## Full version

For lessons, the tracker, the arthroplasty guide's introduction and the blog.

### Heading

How much ache is acceptable?

### Body

When a joint hurts, it is natural to read every ache as a sign of harm. Pain
deserves attention, but it does not always measure damage to the joint
[CLAIM-NEW-3]. Physical therapists often use a simple guideline to help people
keep moving without guessing.

**Rate the ache from 0 to 10.** 0 is no pain at all. 10 is the worst pain you
can imagine.

**During the activity: up to about 5 out of 10.** A mild to moderate ache that
stays at 5 or below is generally acceptable while you exercise
[CLAIM-C02]. If it climbs above 5, ease off: go slower, use less weight, or
stop that exercise for today.

**The next day: back to your usual.** Your usual is how the joint normally
feels on an ordinary day. Any extra ache should settle back to that within
about 24 hours [CLAIM-C02]. If it has, that amount of activity was a dose your
joint recovered from.

**If it has not settled after 24 hours,** do less next time. Shorten the session,
lower the weight, or do fewer sets, and build back up once the joint is back to
its usual. One harder day is information about the dose, not a sign you should
stop moving.

This is a practical guideline, not a hard limit, and it is not a promise that
nothing can go wrong. It helps you find a starting dose and adjust it.

### When the guideline does not apply

- **After surgery, your surgeon's or physical therapist's protocol comes
  first.** Use the pain levels and limits they give you, even if they differ
  from these.
- **A hot, swollen joint, or one that catches and gets stuck, is not an
  "acceptable ache".** Stop and see a clinician (see the red-flag box).
- **Sharp, sudden or a new kind of pain** is a reason to stop that activity,
  even if it scores below 5.
- **Rheumatoid or other inflammatory arthritis:** this guideline was written
  with osteoarthritis in mind. Ask your rheumatology team whether it fits you
  (see the inflammatory arthritis note).
- **It does not replace advice from a clinician who knows your history.** If
  they have told you something different, follow them.

## Tracker adjustment rules

For A4, replacing the source's "Red flags & adjustments" box. The second rule
replaces the unsourced "reduce your volume by 50%" (see `copy-fixes.md`, F24).

**Adjusting your next session**

- **Ache above 5 out of 10 during the activity:** next time, lower the
  intensity (slower, lighter) or shorten the time.
- **Not back to your usual after 24 hours:** next time, cut the amount back. A
  reasonable starting point is about half the time, distance or sets you did,
  then build back up over the following sessions once the joint has settled.
  *This is Randy's clinical guidance from practice, not a figure from a
  research study.*
- **Hot, swollen, catching or getting stuck, or any sign in the red-flag box:**
  stop and contact a clinician.

## Words to avoid with this guideline

- "safe signal", "safe zone", "the activity is safe": use "generally
  acceptable".
- "perfect loading dose", "exact dose": use "a dose your joint recovered from".
- "pain means your joint is getting fed" or "rebuilding": remove. The ache is a
  signal to watch, not proof of repair.
- "should never exceed 5" (from the source tool): contradicts "up to 5 is
  acceptable". Use the wording above.
- "push through": never.
