# Post-surgery caveat

Sits above every post-surgery exercise: at the top of the Post-Arthroplasty
Loading Guide, above each exercise block in it, in the guide delivery email,
and above any post-surgery section of a lesson. Arthroplasty means joint
replacement surgery; say "joint replacement" in reader copy.

The caveat makes no research claim, so it carries no claim ID. Any evidence
sentence that follows it (for example the Wei 2024 results, C08, Promising)
comes from `copy-fixes.md` (F24 to F26), not from here.

## Full version (top of the guide, top of a lesson section)

### Heading

Before you start: your surgical team comes first

### Body

This guide is for people who have had a knee or hip replaced, or who are
preparing for it. It is educational. It is not your rehab plan.

- **Use these exercises only after your surgeon or physical therapist has given you the go-ahead for them.** Every operation and every recovery is different, and
  the timing that suits one person can be too early for another.
- **Your team's protocol wins.** If their instructions differ from anything
  here, including the acceptable pain guide, follow theirs.
- **Take this guide to your next appointment** and ask which parts fit your
  stage of recovery.

**Get help now** (call 911 or your local emergency number) for:

- pain, swelling, warmth or tenderness in your calf or the back of your thigh;
- chest pain, or sudden shortness of breath.

After surgery these can be signs of a blood clot, which needs urgent care.

**Contact your surgical team today** if:

- the wound is red, hot, leaking or opening up;
- you feel feverish or unwell;
- the joint suddenly becomes much more swollen or painful;
- you suddenly cannot bend or straighten it as far as before.

## Short version (above each exercise block, and in email)

> **After surgery, check with your team first.** Do these exercises only once
> your surgeon or physical therapist has given you the go-ahead for them, and follow
> their protocol if it differs from this guide. Get help now for calf pain or
> swelling, chest pain or sudden breathlessness. Contact your surgical team
> today for a red, hot or leaking wound, a fever, or a sudden increase in
> swelling or pain.

## One-line version (exercise cards with no room)

> Only once your surgeon or physical therapist has given you the go-ahead. Their protocol
> comes first.

## Rules for copy around this caveat

- Do not call active resistance exercise "superior", "the evidence-based
  default" or a way to "maximise surgical outcomes". See `copy-fixes.md` F24
  to F26 for the replacement.
- Do not call usual rehab "passive". The trials compared resistance exercise
  with standard exercise (C08).
- Do not set week-by-week timelines (for example "by week 6 you should") unless
  the clinical reviewer supplies and approves them.
- Do not use "protection" as the opposite of exercise. The surgical team's
  precautions are part of recovery, not a mistake to correct.
