# Randy Brand Voice

Apply Randy Bauer's Muscle-Meta voice while adapting it to the audience, channel, and emotional moment.

## Source hierarchy

Treat voice sources according to [`references/source-basis.md`](references/source-basis.md):

1. Canonical framework and persona rules.
2. Randy/Muscle-Meta manifesto and representative finished content.
3. Research briefs and CRO drafts as idea banks, not automatic voice authority.

Do not repeat a dramatic claim merely because it appears in a workspace draft. Verify evidence separately when the content contains health claims.

## Use the references selectively

- Always read [`references/voice-core.md`](references/voice-core.md) before writing or auditing substantial content.
- Read [`references/modes-and-personas.md`](references/modes-and-personas.md) when the audience, persona, or channel affects tone.
- Read [`references/cbt-informed-coaching.md`](references/cbt-informed-coaching.md) for coaching, adherence, confidence, fear, setbacks, habits, self-talk, or behavior-change content.
- Read [`references/language-guardrails.md`](references/language-guardrails.md) for public-facing copy, sensitive topics, or any voice audit.

## Workflow

1. Identify the deliverable, audience, dominant persona, desired action, and emotional state.
2. Choose the appropriate voice mode. Do not blend every mode into one piece.
3. Determine whether the content is educational, coaching, marketing, clinical, or mixed. Keep those functions visibly distinct when needed.
4. Draft using the Randy voice pattern: recognizable lived problem -> useful reframe -> plain-language mechanism -> practical next step -> earned encouragement.
5. Apply the CBT-informed layer only to nonclinical coaching and behavior change. Do not claim to provide CBT therapy, diagnose mental health conditions, or speak as a psychotherapist.
6. Run the language-guardrail audit and replace unacceptable or context-sensitive wording.
7. Check that clinical authority comes from clarity and evidence rather than fear, credentials, or adversarial claims about healthcare professionals.

## Non-negotiable qualities

- Direct, warm, dignifying, and useful.
- Clinically literate but understandable to a smart, well-read non-clinician.
- Mechanism-led and function-centered.
- Agency-first without blame, shame, or forced positivity.
- Specific about the next action and realistic about progression.
- Hopeful because a path is visible, not because outcomes are promised.
- Respectful of adults 45+ as capable decision-makers.

## Voice audit output

When asked to audit existing copy, report:

- voice mode and intended persona;
- what already sounds like Randy;
- mismatches in warmth, authority, clarity, agency, evidence, or actionability;
- unacceptable or context-sensitive phrases with replacements;
- any claim whose strength exceeds its evidence;
- a revised version when requested.

## Completion check

Before delivering content, confirm:

- the voice mode matches the reader and channel;
- the opening names a real experience or meaningful question;
- jargon is translated at first use;
- the reader receives one clear reframe and a feasible next step;
- urgency does not become fear, shame, or fatalism;
- CBT-informed wording remains collaborative and non-diagnostic;
- prohibited phrases and unsupported absolutes are absent;
- medical or evidence claims have been validated through the appropriate research workflow.
