# Voice Modes and Personas

Choose one dominant mode and one dominant persona. Secondary influences may adjust details but should not make the piece sound inconsistent.

## Channel modes

### Coaching conversation

**Tone:** Collaborative, curious, calm, specific.  
**Purpose:** Help the person examine a belief, choose a useful experiment, and build confidence through evidence from their own actions.  
**Shape:** Reflect -> ask -> reframe -> choose -> plan -> measure.  
**Avoid:** Lecturing, diagnosing, fixing the person, or issuing a long prescription before understanding the barrier.

Read `cbt-informed-coaching.md`.

### Patient or client education

**Tone:** Warm, direct, dignifying.  
**Purpose:** Make a mechanism understandable and show what it changes about the next decision.  
**Shape:** Recognizable experience -> plain-language mechanism -> two or three priorities -> safety context.  
**Reading level:** Generally eighth- to tenth-grade without sounding simplistic.

### Newsletter or email

**Tone:** Personal, belief-building, concise.  
**Purpose:** Reveal one underrecognized distinction and lead to one next step.  
**Shape:** Familiar moment or counterintuitive fact -> why it matters -> encouraging truth -> practical sequence -> one CTA.  
**Style:** One central idea, moderate evidence density, no crowded reference performance.

### Long-form article or lesson

**Tone:** Teacher-coach with clinical depth.  
**Purpose:** Build understanding that changes behavior.  
**Shape:** Question or story -> belief gap -> direct answer -> mechanism -> evidence -> practical application -> limitations -> next step.  
**Rule:** Education comes before persuasion.

### Assessment question or result

**Tone:** Neutral, reassuring, agency-first.  
**Purpose:** Turn an observation into direction without turning a score into an identity.  
**Preferred frame:** “This gives us a starting point” and “Here is the first area to strengthen.”  
**Avoid:** Pass/fail framing, moral labels, alarmist risk language, or certainty beyond the assessment's validity.

### Landing page or offer

**Tone:** Clear, confident, specific, ethically persuasive.  
**Purpose:** Show the reader a relevant gap, a credible mechanism, and a concrete pathway.  
**Shape:** Recognized problem -> overlooked mechanism -> specific benefit -> what the pathway includes -> appropriate CTA.  
**Avoid:** Secret-withholding accusations, countdown pressure, guaranteed outcomes, exaggerated prevalence, and fear as the primary conversion mechanism.

### Social post or short video

**Tone:** Conversational, vivid, focused.  
**Purpose:** Disrupt one belief and give one useful action.  
**Shape:** Hook -> one explanation -> one example -> one next step.  
**Rule:** Keep one metaphor and one claim. Do not compress away qualifiers necessary for safety.

### Clinician or professional communication

**Tone:** Peer-to-peer, precise, efficient.  
**Purpose:** Support a decision or explain a mechanism.  
**Style:** Technical terms are acceptable, but define uncommon abbreviations and separate evidence from clinical interpretation.  
**Avoid:** Consumer marketing hooks, antagonism toward other clinicians, or implying consensus where none exists.

### Caregiver or family communication

**Tone:** Respectful, practical, autonomy-preserving.  
**Purpose:** Help another person support capability without controlling or infantilizing the adult receiving help.  
**Prefer:** “Support,” “invite,” “make the next step easier,” and “preserve choice.”  
**Avoid:** “Make them,” “force,” “take over,” or talking about an adult as though they are absent.

## Persona adjustments

### Rebuilders

Adults returning after injury, surgery, illness, hospitalization, or immobilization.

- **Voice:** Urgent but hopeful; evidence-anchored; recovery-sequenced.
- **Core concern:** “Will I get back?”
- **Emphasize:** A clear recovery path, protected loading, rebuilding signals, and measurable functional wins.
- **Avoid:** Blame for lost capacity, “push through,” and timelines stated as guarantees.
- **Useful line:** “You have lost capacity, but that gives us something specific to rebuild.”

### Longevity Seekers

Adults concerned about independence, balance, cognition, and capable years.

- **Voice:** Warm, dignifying, future-oriented.
- **Core concern:** Loss of autonomy.
- **Emphasize:** Independence, reserve, load capacity, early signals, and actions that preserve options.
- **Avoid:** Patronizing age labels, catastrophic fall or dementia framing, and “be careful” as the whole plan.
- **Useful line:** “Your age gives us context. Your current capacity gives us the starting point.”

### Maintainers and Enhancers

Active adults, recreational athletes, and performance-minded people.

- **Voice:** Peer-to-peer, performance-coded, concise.
- **Core concern:** “Why can I still perform but no longer recover?”
- **Emphasize:** Capacity gaps, recovery architecture, sport-specific function, and measurable tuning.
- **Avoid:** Generic beginner language, excessive reassurance, and age-based limitation.
- **Useful line:** “Your engine still performs. It needs a different recovery and loading strategy now.”

### Preventers and Metabolic Reset

Adults noticing energy, glucose, body-composition, or family-history warning signals.

- **Voice:** Reassuring, action-coded, agency-first.
- **Core concern:** “Is this trajectory becoming disease?”
- **Emphasize:** Early action, data, fuel flexibility, muscle as an active metabolic organ, and small repeatable behaviors.
- **Avoid:** Shame, moralizing food or body size, and implying a laboratory result is destiny.
- **Useful line:** “You are seeing the signal early, which gives you more room to act.”

## Persona crossover

When personas overlap, use the dominant emotional need to choose the opening and the dominant physiological need to choose the mechanism and next action. Do not stack persona labels in reader-facing copy.
