The reframe

Your joint is living tissue, not a tire

Many of us were told that osteoarthritis is “wear and tear”: that joints wear out like car tires, one mile at a time. It is one of the most common beliefs about joints, and one of the least helpful. In a pooled analysis of 17 studies, 3.5% of recreational runners had hip or knee arthritis, compared with 10.2% of non-runners. This is a link, not proof that running prevents arthritis. (Alentorn-Geli 2017)Promising But it does not fit the idea that every step wears the joint down.

Exercise is medicine for the joint. International guidelines from OARSI (2019) list arthritis education and structured land-based exercise as core treatments for knee and hip osteoarthritis, recommended for essentially everyone with the condition. (Bannuru 2019)Established

Who it is for

Three kinds of people this course was written for

Living with it

You are managing knee or hip osteoarthritis

You have been told to “keep active”, but every ache makes you wonder whether you are doing harm. You want a plain way to judge how much is enough, and how much is too much.

After surgery

You have had a knee replaced

You want to understand how strength work fits into recovery once your team says it is time, and what the research on it does and does not show. Please read the note below first.

Still playing

You are active and want to keep playing

Pickleball, tennis, golf, hiking or running. Your knee or hip has started to talk back, and you want a way to read it and adjust your week, instead of guessing or stopping.

After surgery, check with your team first. Do these exercises only once your surgeon or physical therapist has given you the go-ahead for them, and follow their protocol if it differs from this course. Get help now for calf pain or swelling, chest pain or sudden breathlessness. Contact your surgical team today for a red, hot or leaking wound, a fever, or a sudden increase in swelling or pain.

Rheumatoid or other inflammatory arthritis? This course was built around osteoarthritis. Inflammatory arthritis flares differently, so use it alongside your rheumatology team’s advice, not in place of it, and ask them whether the acceptable pain guide fits you. During a flare, check with them before loading a hot or swollen joint.

What it covers

Three short lessons

Each lesson is a short page you scroll through at your own pace, with a video from Randy of about two minutes, a short interactive, a tracker and one small step to try that week.

  1. Lesson 1Video: about 2 minutes

    Why rest is often not the help it seems

    When a joint hurts, the natural instinct is to keep it still. Cartilage has no blood supply of its own. Its nutrients reach it mainly from the fluid inside the joint, and lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that movement and loading help move that fluid into the cartilage. (Mauck 2003; Graham 2017)Unproven-Bridge

    Short rest is the right call for a hot, swollen joint, during a flare your clinician is managing, or when your surgical team has asked for it. The problem is long stretches of rest as the everyday default. Your small step: five minutes of gentle movement a day.

  2. Lesson 2Video: about 2 minutes

    The acceptable pain guide

    Pain is your body’s alarm system. With arthritis, a moderate ache during exercise does not by itself mean the joint is being damaged. (Bedson 2008; Bricca 2019; Fransen 2015)Promising

    You learn a practical guideline many clinicians use: an ache up to about 5 out of 10 during activity, back to your usual by the next day. It is clinical guidance, not a tested safety limit, and the lesson shows you what to do when a session does not settle. Unproven-Bridge Your small step: chair sit-to-stands, rated with the guide.

  3. Lesson 3Video: about 2 minutes

    Choosing your exercise

    There is no single correct exercise for everyone. In one network analysis of 39 trials in knee arthritis, all five exercise types studied helped, and different types ranked highest for different outcomes. These rankings are options studied, not proof that one type is best for you. (Mo 2023)Promising

    The type you will keep doing matters most. Your small step: put three 15-minute sessions of your chosen exercise on the calendar for the coming week.

The evidence

What the course rests on

Every claim in the course carries a grade. Established means human trials or guideline consensus. Promising means human studies that show a link or early, lower-certainty results. Unproven-Bridge means lab, animal or mechanism research, or clinical guidance that has not been tested as a rule.

  1. International guidelines from OARSI (2019) list arthritis education and structured land-based exercise as core treatments for knee and hip osteoarthritis, recommended for essentially everyone with the condition. Established

    Bannuru et al., 2019. Osteoarthritis Cartilage. PMID 31278997

  2. A Cochrane review of 54 trials found that land-based exercise reduced knee arthritis pain by about 12 points and improved function by about 10 points on a 0 to 100 scale right after treatment. Smaller benefits (about 6 points for pain and 3 for function) were still present 2 to 6 months later. Established

    Fransen et al., 2015. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. PMID 25569281

  3. In one network analysis of 39 trials in knee arthritis, all five exercise types studied helped. Cycling ranked highest for one pain score and walking distance, water exercise for another pain score, yoga for stiffness and daily function, and resistance training for overall symptoms. These rankings are options studied, not proof that one type is best for you. Promising

    Mo et al., 2023. Orthop J Sports Med. PMID 37346776

  4. A review of 14 trials (880 people after knee replacement) found that lower-limb resistance exercise led to somewhat better walking speed, a slightly faster sit-stand-walk test (about 1 second), more leg strength and a little more knee bend (about 3 degrees) than standard exercise, measured right after the program. The overall quality of the evidence was low. Promising

    Wei et al., 2024. BMC Musculoskelet Disord. PMID 39267026

  5. In a study of people with arthritis, fear of movement was more closely linked with how well they managed daily activities than their X-ray findings were. Avoiding activity is also linked with weaker muscles and more difficulty with daily tasks. Promising

    Heuts et al., 2004. Pain. PMID 15275772. Holla et al., 2014. J Behav Med. PMID 24841177

  6. In a pooled analysis of 17 studies, 3.5% of recreational runners had hip or knee arthritis, compared with 10.2% of non-runners and 13.3% of competitive runners. The study could not tell whether running caused the difference. Promising

    Alentorn-Geli et al., 2017. J Orthop Sports Phys Ther. PMID 28504066

  7. Pain is your body’s alarm system. With arthritis, a moderate ache during exercise does not by itself mean the joint is being damaged. Promising

    Bedson et al., 2008. BMC Musculoskelet Disord. PMID 18764949. Bricca et al., 2019. Arthritis Care Res (Hoboken). PMID 30320965. Fransen et al., 2015. Cochrane Database Syst Rev. PMID 25569281

  8. Exercise that loads the knee does not appear to harm cartilage in people with or at risk of knee arthritis. One small trial found signs of healthier cartilage content after 4 months of exercise. Exercise has not been shown to regrow lost cartilage. Unproven-Bridge

    Roos et al., 2005. Arthritis Rheum. PMID 16258919. Bricca et al., 2019. Arthritis Care Res (Hoboken). PMID 30320965

  9. Cartilage has no blood supply of its own. Its nutrients reach it mainly by moving in from the fluid inside the joint. Lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that joint movement and loading help move that fluid, and the larger molecules it carries, into the cartilage. Unproven-Bridge

    Mauck et al., 2003. J Biomech Eng. PMID 14618919. Graham et al., 2017. Osteoarthritis Cartilage. PMID 28888900

  10. A common clinical guide is that an ache during exercise is acceptable if it stays moderate (many clinicians use about 5 out of 10) and settles within 24 hours. This is clinical guidance, not a tested safety limit for arthritis. Unproven-Bridge

    Clinical guidance used by physical therapists. No study has tested it as a safety limit for arthritis, so no citation is given.

Questions

Common questions

Is running bad for my knees?

Not for most people who run for fun, based on what we know so far. Recreational runners had lower rates of hip and knee arthritis than non-runners in one pooled analysis: 3.5% compared with 10.2%. That is a link, not proof that running protects the joint. (Alentorn-Geli 2017)Promising If running is part of your life, the acceptable pain guide can help you judge your dose.

Why does my joint feel better when I move it?

Cartilage has no blood supply of its own. Its nutrients reach it mainly from the fluid inside the joint (synovial fluid), and lab studies on cartilage samples suggest that movement and loading help move that fluid into the cartilage. (Mauck 2003; Graham 2017)Unproven-Bridge That fluid exchange is part of how the joint’s metabolism works.

Should I take ibuprofen or acetaminophen instead of exercising?

It doesn’t have to be one or the other. OARSI guidelines list exercise as a core treatment for knee and hip osteoarthritis. (Bannuru 2019)Established Medicines depend on the joint and on your other health conditions, so talk them through with your doctor. (Bannuru 2019)Established

I’ve had a knee replacement. Surely I should rest it?

Rest has a place in the early days, and your surgical team’s plan comes first. After that, a review of 14 trials found that resistance exercise for the legs led to somewhat better walking speed, more leg strength and a little more knee bend than standard exercise, though the evidence was low quality. (Wei 2024)Promising Ask your team when resistance work fits your recovery.

What is the best exercise for my arthritis?

In one analysis of trials in knee arthritis, all five types of exercise studied helped, including cycling, water exercise and yoga. (Mo 2023)Promising The best one is the one you will keep doing.

Is this course treatment?

No. Load as Medicine is an educational course, not treatment. It does not diagnose, and it does not replace your own clinician. After joint surgery, your surgeon’s or physical therapist’s protocol comes first.

Will the course give my joint a number or a rating?

No. Neither the course nor the free joint-load check gives you a percentage or a score. The check gives you the outcome of a short safety screen, a plain reading of your answers about movement and load, and one next step.

Medical disclaimer

Everything on this site is educational. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment, and it is not a substitute for evaluation by a qualified clinician.

The assessments and calculators here estimate, they do not measure. Field tests carry meaningful error compared with laboratory testing, and normative bands describe populations rather than individuals. Treat any result as a starting point for a conversation with your own clinician.

Talk with your physician or physical therapist before beginning a new exercise program — particularly if you have a heart condition, chest pain on exertion, uncontrolled blood pressure, a recent surgery, or have been inactive for some time. Stop any test or session for chest pain, dizziness, or unusual shortness of breath.

Load as Medicine is an educational course, not treatment. After joint surgery, your surgeon’s or physical therapist’s protocol comes first. Stop and contact a clinician if a joint is hot, red or swollen with a fever, gives way or gets stuck, cannot take weight after a fall, or if your calf hurts or swells after a joint replacement.
