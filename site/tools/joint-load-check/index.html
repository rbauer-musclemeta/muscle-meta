The joint-load check

A free check for adults 50 and over living with knee or hip arthritis, including people after a joint replacement and players who want to keep playing. It starts with a short safety screen, then asks how you think about movement and how your joint handles load. You get a plain reading of your answers and one next step. There is no score.

A different way to see it

Your joint is living tissue, not a tire

Draft wording, pending clinical review

Many people are told their joints "wear out" with use. It is an easy picture to believe, and it is worth a second look.

Cartilage has no blood supply of its own. It relies on the joint moving and taking weight, then easing off, to move fluid in and out. That is one reason joint specialists now recommend exercise as a first treatment for knee and hip osteoarthritis.

Short rest during a bad flare is fine. Long stretches of avoiding movement are what tend to leave a joint weaker and stiffer.

Two practical guidelines while you build

These are practical guidelines that many physical therapists use, not hard rules:

  • During activity, joint ache stays at 5 out of 10 or below.
  • By the next day, any extra ache has settled back to your usual level within 24 hours.

Follow your surgeon's or clinician's protocol if there is one. A hot, swollen or stuck joint needs a clinician, not a guideline. Neither rule replaces advice from someone who knows your history.
